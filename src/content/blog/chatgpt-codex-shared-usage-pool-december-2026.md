OpenAI just announced a significant change that will affect every ChatGPT user: starting in December 2026, ChatGPT and Codex will share a single token-based usage allowance. If you're on Plus, Pro, or Business plans, this is something you need to understand before it rolls out.

I've been tracking OpenAI's pricing changes for years, and this shift signals a fundamental change in how they're thinking about their product. Let me break down what's happening and how to prepare.

## What's Changing?

Currently, ChatGPT and Codex (OpenAI's agentic coding assistant) have separate usage limits. You could max out your ChatGPT usage and still have full access to Codex, or vice versa.

Starting December 2026, that's over. Both products will draw from the same token pool.

Here's the breakdown by plan:

| Plan | Current Model | December 2026 Model |
|------|--------------|---------------------|
| Plus ($20/mo) | Separate limits | Shared token pool |
| Pro ($200/mo) | Separate limits | Shared token pool |
| Business | Per-seat separate | Shared per-seat pool |

The key thing to understand: **not all tokens are created equal**. More advanced models and complex tasks consume tokens faster than simple chat interactions.

## Why Is OpenAI Doing This?

This change comes as OpenAI integrates Codex's agentic capabilities directly into the main ChatGPT experience. If you've been following the AI coding space, you know that Codex can now:

- Navigate entire codebases autonomously
- Run tests and iterate on failures
- Create pull requests
- Execute multi-step development tasks

Keeping these products separate doesn't make sense when the underlying experience is converging.

But there's also a business angle here. OpenAI is clearly trying to:

1. **Simplify billing**: One pool is easier to explain than two separate systems
2. **Encourage upgrades**: Heavy users will hit limits faster, pushing them toward higher tiers
3. **Prepare for GPT-6.1 Terra**: The new model that just launched with halved API prices needs a sustainable consumption model

## What Stays Free?

Not everything counts against your allowance. OpenAI confirmed that **instant chat will not draw from the shared pool**.

This means quick back-and-forth conversations—the "what's the capital of France" type queries—won't eat into your budget. But anything involving:

- Code generation and editing
- Complex reasoning tasks
- Agentic workflows
- Multi-step problem solving

All of that hits your token allowance.

## How to Track Your Usage

OpenAI is adding a unified usage dashboard at **Settings → Usage**. You'll be able to see:

- Real-time token consumption
- Breakdown by feature (Chat vs. Codex vs. other tools)
- Historical usage patterns
- Projected reset dates

If you're already close to your limits, you'll want to start monitoring this now to understand your baseline before December.

## What Happens When You Hit the Limit?

You have three options when you exhaust your token allowance:

1. **Wait for reset**: Your allocation refreshes at the start of your billing cycle
2. **Upgrade your plan**: Move from Plus to Pro, or Pro to a higher tier
3. **Buy credits**: One-time token purchases (pricing not yet announced)

The credit system is new and suggests OpenAI is moving toward a more granular consumption-based model. This aligns with their API pricing structure and could be a preview of future subscription changes.

## How This Affects Different Users

### Casual ChatGPT Users

If you mainly use ChatGPT for research, writing assistance, and occasional coding help, you probably won't notice much difference. The limits are designed to be generous for typical usage.

### Power Users

If you're running multiple Codex projects, using ChatGPT for extensive writing, or relying on the tools for daily work, start tracking your usage now. You may need to:

- Consolidate tasks to be more efficient
- Consider upgrading your plan
- Budget your usage across projects

### Development Teams

Business plan users should audit how their team uses both products. If you have developers heavily using Codex while other team members use ChatGPT for other work, you're now competing for the same pool.

Consider whether you need:
- More seats
- Higher per-seat allocations
- Dedicated Codex API access separate from ChatGPT

## My Recommendations

Based on my analysis of this change, here's what I'd suggest:

### Before December

1. **Monitor your current usage** in both ChatGPT and Codex separately
2. **Calculate your total** to see if combined limits would be a problem
3. **Review your plan tier** and whether it still makes sense

### After December

1. **Set up usage alerts** (if OpenAI provides them) to avoid surprise limits
2. **Batch complex tasks** rather than spreading them throughout the day
3. **Use instant chat for simple queries** to preserve your token budget for heavy lifting

### For Teams

1. **Audit team usage patterns** across both products
2. **Consider dedicated API access** for high-volume development work
3. **Establish usage guidelines** so one team member doesn't exhaust shared resources

## The Bigger Picture

This change is part of OpenAI's broader strategy of consolidating their product suite. We've seen GPT-6.1 Terra launch with halved API prices, aggressive integration of Codex into ChatGPT, and now unified usage accounting.

For users, this means simpler products but also less flexibility. If you valued having separate pools for different use cases, that's going away.

For OpenAI, it means better visibility into total user consumption and a cleaner path to future pricing models. Don't be surprised if 2027 brings more significant changes to how ChatGPT subscriptions work.

## Timeline Recap

- **Now**: Separate usage limits for ChatGPT and Codex
- **December 2026**: Shared token allowance goes live for Plus, Pro, and Business
- **Unknown**: Credit system launches for one-time purchases

Start preparing now. Check your usage, understand your patterns, and make sure your plan tier will support your needs once the pools merge.

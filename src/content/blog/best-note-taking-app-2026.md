# Best Note-Taking Apps 2026: Obsidian vs Notion vs Roam Research (Compared)

I have notes from 2019 that I still reference weekly. Meeting notes that saved projects. Research collections that became articles. Ideas captured in the middle of the night that turned into actual things.

The right note-taking app becomes an extension of your mind. The wrong one becomes a graveyard of forgotten captures. I've used Obsidian, Notion, and Roam Research extensively—each for over three years—and they serve fundamentally different purposes.

Here's what I've learned about which one fits which kind of brain.

## Quick Verdict

**Obsidian** — Best for privacy-focused knowledge builders who want local control and linking power.

**Notion** — Best for teams and people who want an all-in-one workspace combining notes, tasks, and databases.

**Roam Research** — Best for researchers and networked thinkers who prioritize daily notes and backlinks.

## Obsidian: The Local-First Powerhouse

Obsidian stores your notes as plain markdown files on your computer. No cloud required. No subscription for core features. Your notes are yours forever.

### What Makes Obsidian Different

**Your files, your control.** Notes are markdown files in a folder. Open them in any text editor. Back them up however you want. No proprietary format, no lock-in.

**Privacy is real.** Nothing leaves your device unless you choose sync. For journalists, researchers, lawyers, anyone handling sensitive information—this matters.

**The plugin ecosystem is incredible.** Calendar integration, spaced repetition, kanban boards, graph analysis, AI assistance—community plugins extend Obsidian into almost anything. Over 1,000 plugins available.

**Linking creates a second brain.** Bi-directional links connect ideas. The graph view visualizes connections. Over time, your notes become a knowledge network.

**Customization is deep.** Themes, CSS snippets, custom hotkeys—Obsidian adapts to your workflow completely.

### My Obsidian Setup

After three years, my vault structure:

```
/Daily Notes
/Projects (one folder per project)
/Resources (reference material)
/People (notes about individuals)
/Fleeting (quick captures to process)
/Templates
```

I use the Dataview plugin for dynamic queries, Calendar for daily notes, and QuickAdd for capturing ideas. Total customization time: Maybe 20 hours over three years, but it's exactly what I need.

### Where Obsidian Falls Short

**Collaboration is awkward.** Sharing requires third-party sync and loses many features. This is a solo tool.

**Mobile app is slower.** Works, but not as smooth as Notion. Sync requires paid add-on or DIY setup.

**Learning curve is real.** To get full value, you need to understand markdown, build systems, configure plugins. Not plug-and-play.

**No native databases.** Plugins add database-like features, but it's not Notion's polish.

## Notion: The All-in-One Workspace

Notion tries to be everything: notes, tasks, databases, wikis, project management. For many people, it succeeds.

### What Makes Notion Popular

**Databases are genuinely powerful.** Create tables, boards, calendars, galleries—all views of the same data. Link databases together. Filter, sort, formula support. It's a flexible data layer for organizing anything.

**Collaboration is seamless.** Share pages, comment, edit together, permission controls. Notion works for teams in a way Obsidian and Roam don't.

**Templates accelerate everything.** Community templates for project management, CRMs, content calendars, personal dashboards. Start from templates, customize to fit.

**Low learning curve.** The basics work immediately. Drag, drop, type, done. Depth exists but isn't required.

**Mobile app is excellent.** Polished, fast, full-featured. Notion on mobile is genuinely useful.

### My Notion Setup

I use Notion for:
- Collaborative projects with others
- Databases that need relational structure
- Content I share publicly
- Quick captures when mobile

My personal knowledge base remains in Obsidian. Notion handles shared contexts.

### Where Notion Falls Short

**Linking is basic.** Backlinks exist but aren't first-class. No graph view. The networked thinking experience is weaker.

**Cloud dependency.** Your notes require Notion's servers. Offline is limited. If Notion shuts down, migration is work.

**Privacy concerns.** Your notes live on Notion's servers. Fine for most uses, concerning for sensitive content.

**Can become cluttered.** The flexibility that enables everything also enables chaos. Without discipline, Notion workspaces become messy.

**Performance at scale.** Large Notion workspaces slow down. Heavy databases can lag.

## Roam Research: The Networked Thought Pioneer

Roam pioneered the modern note-taking-with-backlinks movement. It's built around daily notes, bi-directional linking, and block references.

### What Makes Roam Unique

**Daily notes as entry point.** Open Roam, you're in today's daily note. Capture everything there, link outward. No folder hierarchies to navigate.

**Block references are powerful.** Reference any individual bullet point anywhere else. Changes sync everywhere. Build ideas from fragments.

**Backlinks create networks.** Every link automatically creates a backlink. Ideas connect organically. Emergent structure from bottom-up linking.

**Query language.** Search and filter blocks programmatically. Power users build dynamic views without plugins.

**Research workflow fit.** Academic researchers often find Roam's model matches how they think about interconnected ideas.

### The Roam Philosophy

Roam encourages non-hierarchical thinking. Instead of organizing into folders, you link and let structure emerge. Daily capture, weekly review, let backlinks surface connections you didn't plan.

This works brilliantly for some brains. It's confusing for others.

### Where Roam Falls Short

**Expensive.** $15/month or $165/year with no free tier. Significant commitment to evaluate.

**No offline.** Cloud-only with limited offline. Roam's 2023 struggles raised availability concerns.

**Performance issues.** Large graphs slow down. Roam has historically had speed problems with heavy use.

**Competitors caught up.** Obsidian and others adopted Roam's innovations. The unique value proposition narrowed.

**Development pace slowed.** After initial rapid development, Roam's feature evolution has been inconsistent.

## Best Note-Taking App by Use Case

### Best for Students: Obsidian or Notion

**Obsidian if:** You want free, own your notes forever, and will invest time in setup.

**Notion if:** You want templates, easy mobile access, and don't need deep linking.

**Setup tip:** Either way, create a system for lecture notes, readings, and projects. Don't just dump everything in one place.

### Best for Researchers: Obsidian or Roam

**Obsidian if:** Privacy matters, you have large archives, you want plugin flexibility.

**Roam if:** You embrace daily notes, love block references, and budget allows.

**Setup tip:** Use backlinks heavily. Connect papers to concepts to questions to notes. Let the graph reveal connections.

### Best for Teams: Notion

**Why:** No contest. Notion handles team collaboration in ways the others don't. Shared workspaces, permissions, comments, real-time editing.

**Setup tip:** Create clear workspace organization before adding team members. Chaos is hard to fix later.

### Best for Personal Knowledge Management: Obsidian

**Why:** Local files, powerful linking, plugin ecosystem, free. Best for long-term knowledge building.

**Setup tip:** Start simple. Daily notes, a few folders, backlinks. Complexity can grow over time.

### Best for Privacy-Sensitive Work: Obsidian

**Why:** Local-first, end-to-end encrypted sync (optional), no cloud requirement. Your notes never leave your control.

**Setup tip:** Use local sync (Syncthing) or Obsidian Sync for cross-device access without third-party cloud exposure.

## Migration Considerations

### To Obsidian

- Export markdown works well
- Rebuild links manually (tools help)
- Expect 2-4 hours for medium vaults

### To Notion

- Import works but formatting can break
- Databases won't migrate cleanly from markdown
- Plan for significant restructuring

### From Roam

- JSON export preserves structure
- Community tools convert to Obsidian format
- Block references won't fully transfer

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use multiple note apps?

Yes, many people do. Common: Obsidian for personal knowledge, Notion for team collaboration. The key is clear boundaries—don't let notes scatter unpredictably across apps.

### Which has the best mobile app?

Notion is the most polished mobile experience. Obsidian mobile works but is slower. Roam mobile is functional but basic.

### Is the graph view actually useful?

For most people: occasionally interesting, rarely essential. It helps visualize connections and find orphan notes. Power users leverage it more; casual users can ignore it.

### How do I choose between local and cloud?

Local (Obsidian) if: privacy matters, you want ownership, you'll handle backups. Cloud (Notion/Roam) if: you prioritize convenience, need collaboration, trust the provider.

### What about alternatives like Logseq, Capacities, or Reflect?

All worth evaluating. Logseq is an open-source Roam-like. Capacities adds object-oriented organization. Reflect is polished and AI-integrated. The market is healthy with options.

## The Bottom Line

After years with all three:

**Obsidian** is my primary tool. Local files mean I own my notes forever. The plugin ecosystem provides everything I need. The investment in setup paid off.

**Notion** handles collaboration and shared contexts. When working with others or building public resources, Notion is unmatched.

**Roam** I've largely left. Not because it's bad—it pioneered concepts I use daily in Obsidian. But competitors caught up, pricing is high, and I prefer local-first.

My recommendation: **Start with Obsidian** if you're committed to long-term knowledge building. **Start with Notion** if you need collaboration or want the easiest path. **Consider Roam** only if its specific workflow matches exactly how your brain works.

The best note-taking app is the one you'll actually use consistently. Features matter less than finding a tool that fits your thinking style.
